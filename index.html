Who we are

About the Lab

The laboratory carries out experimental and analytical research on the mechanical behaviour of engineering materials and components.

Its work covers crack initiation and growth, fatigue and creep, contact and wear, and full-field stress and strain measurement using photoelasticity and digital image correlation.

The group combines classical experimental mechanics with modern tools such as 3D-printed specimens and image-based measurement.

What we work on

Research Areas

01

Fracture Mechanics

Crack behaviour, stress intensity factors and fracture in ideal and non-ideal cracks.

02

Fatigue & Crack Growth

Fatigue crack propagation in metallic alloys, including the effect of perforations.

03

Machine Design

Design and reliability of mechanical components under service loads.

04

Tribology

Friction, wear and lubrication at contacting surfaces.

05

Experimental Stress Analysis

Measuring stress and strain fields in loaded components and models.

06

Photoelasticity & DIC

Full-field optical methods: photoelastic fringe analysis and digital image correlation.

Equipment

Facilities

Photoelasticity

Polariscope setup for visualising and measuring stress fields in transparent models.

Creep Testing

Long-duration testing of material deformation under sustained load and temperature.

Digital Image Correlation

Camera-based, non-contact measurement of full-field displacement and strain.

3D Printing

Additive manufacturing of specimens, fixtures and photoelastic models.

Fatigue Testing

Cyclic loading of specimens for fatigue life and crack growth studies.
